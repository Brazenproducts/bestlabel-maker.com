Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between thermal and laminated labels?

Thermal labels print using heat (no ink needed) but can fade over time, especially in sunlight or heat. Laminated labels (like Brother TZe tape) have a clear protective layer over the print that resists water, heat, UV, and abrasion — they last 5-8+ years even in tough conditions. For permanent labeling, choose laminated. For temporary or indoor labels, thermal is cheaper. Read our full comparison →

Are label maker tapes universal?

No — each brand uses proprietary cartridges. Brother uses TZe tape, DYMO uses D1 or LetraTag tape, and thermal printers use specific label rolls. The good news: third-party compatible tapes are available for Brother and DYMO at 50-70% less than official tape. They work fine for most uses.

Can I print shipping labels with a label maker?

Basic handheld label makers (Brother, DYMO LetraTag) can't print shipping labels — they only print narrow tape. For shipping labels, you need a thermal label printer like the DYMO LabelWriter 450 or Rollo that prints 4x6 inch labels and connects to your computer.

How long do label maker labels last?

Laminated labels (Brother TZe): 5-8+ years indoors, 3-5 years outdoors. They resist water, heat, and UV. Thermal labels (NIIMBOT, Phomemo): 1-3 years indoors, months outdoors. Heat and sunlight cause faster fading. DYMO LetraTag: 2-4 years indoors.

Do I need Bluetooth on my label maker?

Bluetooth lets you design labels on your phone with more fonts, symbols, templates, and even photos than any built-in keyboard offers. It's great for decorative labels and batch printing. But if you just need quick text labels (file folders, storage bins), a keyboard model is actually faster — no app to open, no phone to find.
